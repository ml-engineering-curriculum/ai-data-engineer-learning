# Exercise 04 — GDPR / CCPA Lawful Basis and the Admit Function

**Estimated effort:** 2 hours
**Chapter dependency:** [Chapter 04 — The Privacy and Copyright Substrate](../04-gdpr-ccpa-and-dsm-article-4.md)

## Objective

Translate a mixed-source training corpus's legal posture into an executable pipeline control — the per-item **admit function** from Chapter 04 — plus the per-source lawful-basis record that legal signs. You will walk the three legal regimes (GDPR, CCPA/CPRA, EU DSM Article 4) and show, at the item level, which switch each regime throws.

## Prerequisites

- You have read Chapter 04.
- You have skimmed GDPR Articles 5, 6, 9, 13, 14, 22; CCPA/CPRA on sale/sharing; EU DSM Copyright Directive Article 4.
- You have reviewed one admit-style filter pattern (even a simple `allowlist / denylist` gate counts).
- You understand the opt-out contract shape from `mod-102-data-sourcing-licensing-and-provenance` even though you have not read that module yet — you can reason about robots.txt per RFC 9309 and ai.txt as signalling mechanisms.

## Problem statement

Your organisation is assembling a 100 GB instruction-tuning corpus from four source categories:

1. **First-party product chat logs** — user conversations with an opted-in beta cohort who agreed to training use in the product T&Cs. Contains PII (names, email addresses, occasional phone numbers) and occasional special-category content (health questions, political views).
2. **Public-web crawl of permissively-licensed technical content** — crawled under your pipeline's crawl contract. Some sites publish `ai.txt` opt-outs; some have `robots.txt` `Disallow: /` for AI crawlers; some have per-page `<meta name="robots" content="noai">` headers; C2PA Content Credentials appear sporadically.
3. **Licensed partner dataset of StackExchange-like Q&A content** — licensed under a written contract that permits training use with attribution.
4. **Synthetic preference pairs generated by distilling a teacher model** on prompts derived from (1), (2), and (3).

Assume the user base is global, including EU and California residents.

Your job is to build the lawful-basis record and the admit function that enforces it.

## Starter guidance

- Treat GDPR Article 6 as the per-item question: *what is the lawful basis for this specific piece of personal data?* For first-party chat, the basis is usually consent (6(1)(a)) or contract (6(1)(b)); for crawled public web, legitimate interests (6(1)(f)) is the common claim but requires a documented LIA.
- Article 9 special categories require a 9(2) exception. In the first-party-chat case, explicit consent (9(2)(a)) is the usual route *if* the consent explicitly covers special categories; otherwise the special-category items get filtered.
- CCPA/CPRA sale-sharing opt-out via GPC is signalled per request. Propagate the status alongside the user record.
- DSM Article 4(3) opt-out is per-source: respect `ai.txt`, `robots.txt`, `<meta robots noai>`, and C2PA opt-out tags. Your admit function checks each.
- Synthetic data inherits restrictions. A synthetic preference pair derived from (1) inherits the (1) consent scope; a synthetic pair derived from a (2) source that was opted out at crawl time should never have been generated.
- Flag US fair-use posture with `<!-- needs-research: ... -->`; do not pretend to opine on it.

## Deliverables

1. **`lawful_basis_record.yaml`** — per-source entry with fields:
   - `source_id`, `category` (first-party / crawl / licensed / synthetic)
   - `gdpr_article_6_basis` and `evidence_pointer` (consent store record, LIA document, contract ID)
   - `gdpr_article_9_handling` (filter, 9(2) exception + basis, or N/A)
   - `ccpa_cpra_posture` (sale, sharing, neither, SPI treatment)
   - `dsm_article_4_posture` (opt-out respected via which signal)
   - `article_14_notice_pointer` (privacy-notice URL / clause)
   - `retention_window` (days)
   - `rights_request_supported` (how a data-subject request against this slice is served)
2. **`admit_function.py` (or pseudocode)** — a function that takes an `Item` and the `CrawlContract` and returns `bool` with a `log_reason`. Must cover: PII without basis, special category without 9(2), CCPA GPC, DSM Article 4 reservation, C2PA opt-out, retention expiry. Include at least 5 explicit log reasons.
3. **`decision_log_sample.jsonl`** — 10 sample admission decisions showing admit and deny cases with reasons.
4. **`subject_request_runbook.md`** — a one-page runbook for how an Article 15 / 17 request is served against the assembled slice.

## Acceptance criteria

- Every one of the four source categories has a lawful-basis record.
- Each GDPR Article 6 basis is tied to a concrete evidence pointer (not "we have a basis" but "LIA-2026-04 §3").
- Article 9 content is handled consistently — either filtered or justified with a 9(2) basis.
- The admit function denies at least one item for each reason: no GDPR basis, Article 9 without exception, GPC signalled, DSM reservation, C2PA opt-out.
- The decision log is machine-parseable (JSONL with stable field names) and every decision carries a timestamp and item SHA.
- The subject-request runbook explains the technical steps to locate a subject's data in the slice, not just a legal recital.
- Nothing in the record claims a US fair-use training posture without a `<!-- needs-research: ... -->` marker handing the decision to counsel.

## Stretch goals

- Extend the admit function to honour a user-level *objection* under GDPR Article 21 recorded in a central objection store, and show it denying previously-admitted items.
- Add a second CCPA tier: handle sensitive personal information (SPI) with limitation-of-use rather than outright denial, tagging the item for a downstream training-time constraint.
- Draft the EDPB-style legitimate-interests assessment (LIA) template for source category (2). Keep it to one page and align the balancing test with the EDPB's three-step framework (purpose, necessity, balancing).
- Add the IETF AI Preferences signal to the admit function as a new opt-out source, gated on signal validity. As of late 2026 the aipref WG has two active Internet-Drafts (`draft-ietf-aipref-vocab`, `draft-ietf-aipref-attach`) and no published RFC yet; check <https://datatracker.ietf.org/wg/aipref/about/> at read time before relying on specific field names.
