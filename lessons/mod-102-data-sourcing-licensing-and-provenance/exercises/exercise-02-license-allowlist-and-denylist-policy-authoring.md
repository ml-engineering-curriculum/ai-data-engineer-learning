# Exercise 02 — Licence Allowlist and Denylist Policy Authoring

**Estimated effort:** 3 hours
**Chapter dependency:** [Chapter 02 — The Data-Licence Matrix and the Allowlist / Denylist Policy](../02-license-matrix-and-allowlist-denylist.md)

## Objective

Author the signed, machine-readable licence policy document that binds your training-data pipeline, plus a test suite that proves the pipeline's `LicenseGate` enforces it. The policy is the compiled form of counsel's current position; the gate is the executable form. Together they are what you point at when asked "under what terms are you allowed to train on this?"

## Prerequisites

- You have read Chapter 02.
- You have reviewed the current Creative Commons 4.0 licence texts (CC-BY, CC-BY-SA, CC-BY-NC, CC-BY-ND, CC0) at <https://creativecommons.org/licenses/>.
- You have read the SPDX Licence List entries for MIT, BSD-2-Clause, BSD-3-Clause, Apache-2.0, GPL-2.0-only, GPL-3.0-only, AGPL-3.0, LGPL-3.0, MPL-2.0 at <https://spdx.org/licenses/>.
- You can write pytest or an equivalent lightweight unit-test framework in Python.

## Problem statement

Your organisation's legal team has set the following posture (treat as the fixture for the exercise; a real one will differ):

- Commercial deployment of trained models is the default. Research-only training is a niche exception.
- CC0, CC-BY, MIT, BSD-2/3, Apache-2.0 are allowlisted for any training objective.
- CC-BY-SA, CC-BY-ND, GPL-family, and AGPL are *conditionally* allowlisted — admission requires a counsel opinion PDF attached to the slice.
- All `-NC` CC variants are denylisted.
- Licensed-partner content is allowlisted under per-contract DLA terms.
- Owned-UGC is allowlisted when the product ToS version is 2024-02-01 or later.
- Synthetic data generated by a third-party model inherits the generator's licence — a separate addendum lists the generators and their training-use postures.
- Default-deny for unlabelled sources.

Your job is to:

1. Author the policy YAML that encodes this posture.
2. Build (or describe pseudocode for) the `LicenseGate` enforcer against the policy.
3. Write a test suite that demonstrates the gate's decisions on a realistic set of inputs, including edge cases.

## Starter guidance

- Reuse the Chapter 02 policy YAML skeleton. Keep field names stable; a brittle-schema policy loses value.
- Where the gate must distinguish between training objectives (e.g. research-only vs commercial), model the objective as a string and parse it in the gate.
- Treat CC-BY-SA, CC-BY-ND, GPL, AGPL as *admit-with-condition*. The "condition" is an artifact path to a counsel opinion PDF; the gate reports the condition and the caller is responsible for attaching the artifact before the slice can release.
- For the generator-licence addendum, author it as a separate YAML referenced by the main policy.
- A spoof case to include in tests: a dataset tagged `CC-BY-NC 4.0` on its Hugging Face card but whose upstream SOURCES list a `CC-BY 4.0` component. The gate should treat the strictest member as controlling — this is a *policy decision*; make it explicit in the policy.
- When in doubt about a licence's SPDX identifier, use the canonical SPDX short form (`CC-BY-NC-4.0`, `GPL-3.0-only`, `MIT`, `Apache-2.0`).

## Deliverables

1. **`policy/data_license_policy_v<DATE>.yaml`** — the signed policy YAML with the Chapter 02 shape: `allowlist`, `allowlist-with-conditions`, `denylist`, `special-case`, `enforcement`. Include at minimum ten explicit allowlist entries, five conditional entries, and five denylist entries.
2. **`policy/generator_license_addendum_v<DATE>.yaml`** — the generator-licence addendum. Enumerate at least three third-party generator models (real or hypothetical) with their training-use postures (e.g. "allows training on outputs for non-competing-product use only").
3. **`license_gate.py`** (code or detailed pseudocode) — the `LicenseGate` class implementing the Chapter 02 interface. Must handle `admit`, `admit-with-condition`, and `deny`, and must log the decision with the policy version and the matched row.
4. **`test_license_gate.py`** — a pytest file with:
   - At least 20 unit cases covering the specific licences in the policy.
   - Edge cases: unlabelled licence; a licence that matches multiple patterns (gate should pick the most specific / strictest); an allowlist-with-condition that resolves to deny when the condition artifact is missing.
   - A snapshot-style test: given an input list of 50 `(licence, objective)` pairs (fixture), assert the aggregate decision histogram matches the policy's intent.
5. **`change_log.md`** — a one-page note explaining how a policy update (e.g. counsel revises CC-BY-SA posture) propagates through the system: which versions change, which slices need re-evaluation, which audit evidence persists.

## Acceptance criteria

- The policy YAML parses cleanly and is self-contained (no undeclared references).
- Every allowlist / denylist entry has a `reason` or `scope` field; no bare tags.
- The gate's decisions on the test fixture are deterministic and derive from the policy; changing the policy changes the test expectations (demonstrate by including one test that intentionally fails if a specific denylist row is removed).
- Default-deny for unlabelled sources is tested.
- The gate logs include the policy version in force at decision time.
- The change-log note explains both forward propagation (new slices use the new policy) and backward propagation (which existing slices must be re-evaluated).
- No unverifiable claim appears in the policy. Legal posture is written as policy text, not as asserted legal conclusions.
- Any question of legal interpretation (e.g. CC-BY-SA onto model weights) is tagged with `<!-- needs-counsel: ... -->` and resolved by `allowlist-with-conditions` rather than by the engineer.

## Stretch goals

- Extend the policy YAML with a `jurisdiction_posture` axis (Chapter 04): the same licence may admit under EU DSM Article 4 and deny under an aggressive-US-fair-use posture, or vice versa.
- Add a `sunset_date` field to allowlist entries that lets counsel schedule a posture change in advance; test that the gate honours the sunset.
- Wire the gate into CI as a precondition on dataset-manifest PRs: a manifest's `license` tag is checked against the current policy before merge.
- Compare your policy to a published one — for example, a GPAI provider's Article 53(1)(c) copyright policy — and note at least two structural differences.
