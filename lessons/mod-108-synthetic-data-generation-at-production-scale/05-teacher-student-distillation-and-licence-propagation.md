# Chapter 05 — Teacher-to-Student Distillation and Licence Propagation

## Why this chapter exists

Teacher-to-student distillation is the most economically important synthetic-data pattern. A strong teacher (GPT-4-class, Claude-class, or a best-in-class open-weight model) is prompted to produce high-quality demonstrations — chain-of-thought traces, calibrated responses, structured outputs — on a seed of problems. The demonstrations are filtered. The filtered corpus is used to SFT or DPO-tune a smaller student model. The result, at a tiny fraction of the pretraining budget, often closes much of the gap between student and teacher on specific capabilities.

The pattern is simple. The failure modes are not.

- **Licensing.** If the teacher's terms of service forbid training a competing model on its outputs, every example in your corpus is a potential licence violation. This has been the single most contested legal question in the 2023–2025 open-model release cycle.
- **Capability-ceiling inheritance.** The student can learn to imitate the teacher's style and surface patterns without learning the underlying capability. Failure mode: the student is confidently wrong in the teacher's voice.
- **Benchmark contamination inheritance.** If the teacher was trained on benchmark `B`, its demonstrations include `B`-shaped content. The student fails a Min-K% Prob audit on `B` even though you never put `B` in the student's training data. See [`mod-106`](../mod-106-benchmark-contamination-and-decontamination/README.md) Chapter 05 and this module's Chapter 10. [[contamination-firewall-on-synthetic]]

This chapter sets the operational pattern and the **licence-propagation ledger** that captures the facts for counsel to render a legal opinion on. The chapter does not render the opinion itself — that is counsel's work.

## The recipe at a glance

```
problem_pool  ─►  teacher_generator  ─►  demonstration_candidate
                                                │
                                                ▼
                                   quality_filter + judge_score
                                                │
                                        ┌───────┴───────┐
                                        ▼               ▼
                                      drop             keep
                                                        │
                                                        ▼
                                           licence_ledger_entry
                                                        │
                                                        ▼
                                                student_training_corpus
```

The problem pool can come from anywhere — a public question set (MATH, GSM8K training split, HumanEval+ training variants where licence permits), a Self-Instruct or Evol-Instruct pool from Chapters 02–03, a human-authored set of problems from [`mod-107`](../mod-107-labeling-operations-and-annotation-programs/README.md). The teacher produces the demonstrations; the filter keeps the ones you want to train on.

## The licence propagation question

Licensing is the first-order concern. The question has two parts:

1. **Does the teacher's licence or terms of service permit using the teacher's outputs to train another model?**
2. **If (1) is yes, with what restrictions does the resulting corpus — and the resulting student model — pass downstream?**

The answer is specific to the teacher, the licence version in effect at generation time, and the downstream use case. Neither you nor this chapter renders the answer. The chapter captures the facts; counsel renders the answer.

### Known reference cases (facts, not legal advice)

Public cases that the licence ledger should be able to point to:

- **OpenAI Terms of Use.** OpenAI's terms have historically restricted use of OpenAI model outputs to train competing LLMs. The exact wording has varied across versions; pin the version in effect at generation time. <!-- needs-research: cite the specific terms-of-use section number and effective date at the time of the run, since the OpenAI terms are revised on cadence. -->
- **Anthropic Usage Policies.** Anthropic's usage policies have similarly addressed use of Claude outputs for model training; pin the version in effect. <!-- needs-research: cite the specific Anthropic usage-policy clause and effective date at the time of the run. -->
- **Alpaca (Taori et al. 2023).** Stanford's Alpaca was fine-tuned on 52k instructions generated by OpenAI's `text-davinci-003` via Self-Instruct. Stanford released the demonstrations and the weights under a non-commercial research licence, explicitly in deference to OpenAI's terms. The Alpaca card is the canonical reference-case documentation. <https://crfm.stanford.edu/2023/03/13/alpaca.html>.
- **Vicuna / ShareGPT.** Vicuna (LMSYS 2023) was fine-tuned on user-shared ChatGPT conversations harvested via the ShareGPT browser extension. The licence posture of this corpus was contested and the ShareGPT dataset was later removed from public distribution. The incident is the textbook example of licence propagation going wrong after the fact.
- **Open-weight teacher cases.** Models released under permissive licences (Apache 2.0, MIT) typically allow use of outputs without downstream restriction, modulo per-model licence clauses. Weights released under non-commercial or research-only licences (various research-release terms) sometimes restrict downstream outputs; read the specific licence. <!-- needs-research: avoid naming specific model releases and claiming their licence posture in the abstract — this changes per release. ### What the ledger captures

The licence ledger is a YAML file in the program package (Chapter 01 §"The program package"):

```yaml
# generator/licence_ledger.yaml
run_id: 2026-01-15T14:00:00Z/distillation-run-7
teacher:
  identity: anthropic:claude-3-5-sonnet-20241022
  provenance: hosted_api
  licence_terms_url: https://www.anthropic.com/legal/aup
  licence_terms_fetched_at: 2026-01-15T13:45:12Z
  licence_terms_sha256: 4a7b...
  licence_terms_version_if_stated: 2024-10-15

student:
  identity: internal:llama-base-7b-sft-run42
  intended_use: internal_product_assistant
  commercial_use: yes

questions_for_counsel:
  - Does the Anthropic Usage Policy as of the fetched date permit using Claude outputs to train an internal, non-API-replacement assistant?
  - Does it permit distribution of the resulting model weights to internal teams only?
  - Does it permit publication of the resulting model externally?

counsel_signoff:
  reviewer: TBD
  reviewer_email: TBD
  reviewed_at: TBD
  opinion_summary: TBD
  opinion_sha256: TBD
  gate_passed: TBD    # true | false | conditional

downstream_restriction_decision:
  derived_corpus_licence: TBD    # e.g., "non-commercial-research" | "internal-only" | "commercial-permitted"
  model_release_eligibility: TBD
```

The ledger is a **release gate**. A distillation run without a signed ledger does not enter student-model training. The engineer (you) authors the questions and captures the facts; counsel fills in the sign-off block.

### The questions-for-counsel shape

Three question families typically land on counsel:

1. **Permission to train.** Does the teacher's licence or terms permit using teacher outputs to train another model?
2. **Permission to distribute.** If (1) is yes, does the licence permit distributing the trained student (internally, externally, commercially)?
3. **Licence propagation.** If (1) and (2) are yes, with what restrictions does the derived corpus (and the student) pass to downstream consumers?

Counsel may answer in binary (yes / no), conditional (yes with named restrictions), or require more information (ask you for a specific additional fact). The ledger supports all three.

## The distillation generation loop

Once the licence ledger clears, the generation loop is mechanically similar to Self-Instruct, with three differences.

### 1. Problem-first, not instruction-first

The pool is a **problem pool** — a set of specific questions, tasks, or prompts the student is expected to handle. The teacher's job is to produce *correct* demonstrations, not to invent new tasks. Diversity concerns are weaker (the pool is already fixed); correctness concerns are stronger.

Problem-pool sources:

- Public question sets (MATH training split, GSM8K training split, HumanEval+ training variants, MBPP, etc. — check per-source licence).
- Internal task catalogue from [`mod-107`](../mod-107-labeling-operations-and-annotation-programs/README.md) or earlier Self-Instruct runs.
- Domain-specific question banks from product analytics (real user queries, licensed appropriately).

### 2. Chain-of-thought is the primary artifact

Distillation typically produces `(prompt, chain-of-thought, final_answer)` triples. The chain-of-thought is where the capability transfers. The final answer alone is usually insufficient signal.

Operating notes:

- **Explicit CoT prompting.** Ask the teacher to "think step by step" or use the native chain-of-thought formatting the teacher model supports.
- **Hidden CoT.** For teachers that produce internal reasoning that is not surfaced in the API response (where applicable), your corpus contains only the surface answer. The student cannot learn the hidden reasoning; adjust the student's expected capability accordingly.
- **CoT-length distribution.** The CoT length distribution predicts the length distribution of the student's reasoning after fine-tuning. Clip extremes.

### 3. Correctness-oriented filters

Different from the diversity-oriented filters of Self-Instruct and Evol-Instruct. The distillation filter stack:

- **Self-consistency check.** Sample the teacher multiple times at `temperature > 0`. Drop problems where the teacher's answers disagree across samples. This is Wang et al. 2022's *Self-Consistency* (arXiv 2203.11171) applied as a filter.
- **Verifier filter.** For problems with a programmatic verifier (math problems with a known answer, code problems with unit tests), run the verifier on the teacher's answer and drop wrong answers.
- **Judge-model score.** For problems without a verifier, use a second, independent judge to score the answer. Drop below a threshold.
- **Refusal filter.** Drop refusal-shaped answers unless they are intentionally in-scope.
- **Length and format sanity.** Standard length-and-format filters.

The filter stack is where distillation's capability transfer is actually set. A noisy filter — one that lets through wrong answers — teaches the student to be confidently wrong.

## The capability-ceiling inheritance problem

Distillation does not transfer capabilities the teacher does not have. More subtly, it can transfer the *appearance* of capabilities the teacher does not reliably have.

Three failure modes:

- **Style imitation without substance.** The student learns to produce long, confident CoT traces that look correct but are not. The teacher's output format is more learnable than its underlying reasoning ability.
- **Error-pattern inheritance.** The teacher's systematic errors (e.g., arithmetic errors in long calculations, specific factual inaccuracies, specific bias patterns) propagate to the student.
- **Truncated distribution inheritance.** The teacher's own refusal and safety behaviour propagates. This may or may not be desirable for your student; be explicit either way.

The filter stack is the mechanism for addressing all three. Verifier filters catch the arithmetic errors. Judge-model filters catch the style-without-substance pattern. Refusal filters make the refusal inheritance explicit.

## Open-weight teacher case

When the teacher is an open-weight model (e.g., a Llama release), the licence question is different in character but not in importance.

- **Model licence governs weight use.** It governs what you can do with the weights; it may or may not address what you can do with the outputs.
- **Teacher's training-data licence.** If the teacher was trained on Common Crawl, parts of Common Crawl may have per-URL restrictions; the teacher's outputs may re-emit such content.
- **Model-card restrictions.** Some open-weight models ship with use-case restrictions (named in the model card). These usually propagate to outputs.

The ledger shape is the same; the counsel questions are different. Fill them in accordingly.

## The distillation manifest

```yaml
# recipes/distillation_config.yaml
teacher:
  model_id: anthropic:claude-3-5-sonnet-20241022
  temperature: 0.2    # low for correctness-oriented generation
  max_tokens: 4096
  licence_ledger_ref: generator/licence_ledger.yaml

problem_pool:
  path: seeds/problem_pool.parquet
  sha256: 7b31...
  source_licences_ref: generator/licence_ledger.yaml
  item_count: 50000

generation:
  samples_per_problem: 1
  self_consistency_samples: 3    # for self-consistency filter
  cot_prompting: explicit

filters:
  self_consistency_agreement_min: 2  # require 2-of-3 agreement
  verifier:
    enabled: true
    verifier_module: verifiers.math_grader
  judge_model:
    model_id: anthropic:claude-3-5-sonnet-20241022    # or an independent judge
    score_threshold: 0.7
  length_range: [10, 4000]
  drop_refusals: true

student:
  intended_identity: internal:llama-base-7b-sft-run42
  intended_use: internal_product_assistant
```

## Where distillation breaks (beyond licensing)

### 1. Teacher-version drift mid-run

Symptom: midway through a 2-day generation run, the hosted teacher model is upgraded. The second half of the corpus is from a subtly different model.

Catch: pin the teacher version with a specific date tag (e.g., `claude-3-5-sonnet-20241022`, not `claude-3-5-sonnet`). An upgrade is a run boundary.

Fix: split the run; update the ledger; counsel re-signs on the new version if the licence terms changed.

### 2. Verifier gaming

Symptom: the teacher's answer passes the verifier but the chain-of-thought is wrong (the right answer with wrong reasoning). The student learns a shortcut.

Catch: on a sample, hand-verify the chain-of-thought, not just the final answer.

Fix: strengthen the judge-model filter to score the reasoning, not just the answer. Or restrict the problem pool to problems where a right answer logically requires right reasoning.

### 3. Benchmark contamination inheritance

Symptom: the teacher is strong on MMLU because MMLU was in its training data. The teacher's distillation outputs on MMLU-adjacent topics include MMLU-shaped content. Your firewall on the student-side training data misses the leak because the leak is in the synthetic surface.

Catch: Chapter 10's firewall on the synthetic output corpus, with the generator-certification contract.

Fix: use a teacher certified against the eval surface, or treat the teacher as untrusted and run the full firewall.

### 4. Hidden user data in teacher outputs

Symptom: a teacher model trained on user data occasionally regurgitates specific user content. Your distillation corpus now contains the regurgitated content; the student learns it.

Catch: PII and sensitive-content scanning on the distillation corpus (coordinate with [`mod-110`](../mod-110-pii-and-privacy-risk-for-training-data/README.md) and `ai-risk-engineer`).

Fix: filter the corpus with PII detection before the student training step. Note that this is detection, not elimination of the risk — the ledger must be clear that the teacher's training data history is upstream of your controls.

## Summary

- Teacher-to-student distillation is the most economically important synthetic pattern and the one with the highest licence-propagation risk.
- The **licence ledger** is a release gate. It captures teacher identity, teacher-licence SHA, downstream intent, counsel questions, counsel sign-off, and derived-corpus licence decision. Counsel renders the opinion; you capture the facts.
- Reference cases to know (facts, not advice): Alpaca's non-commercial release, Vicuna / ShareGPT's post-hoc retraction, the OpenAI and Anthropic outputs clauses.
- Distillation uses a **problem pool** rather than a seed pool, a **chain-of-thought-primary** generation step, and **correctness-oriented filters** (self-consistency, verifier, judge-model).
- The **capability-ceiling inheritance** failure modes — style without substance, error-pattern inheritance, truncated distribution inheritance — are the filter stack's responsibility to catch.
- Contamination inheritance from the teacher is the single biggest hidden failure mode. The generator certification contract in Chapter 10 is the control.
