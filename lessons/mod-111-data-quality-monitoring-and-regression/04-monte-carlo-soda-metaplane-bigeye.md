# Chapter 04 — Monte Carlo, Soda Cloud, Metaplane, Bigeye: The General Data-Observability Landscape

## Why this chapter exists

Chapters 02 and 03 covered the two ML-aware tools — `whylogs` / WhyLabs and Evidently. But most organisations already have a **general data-observability platform** that predates the training-data pipeline: Monte Carlo, Soda Cloud, Metaplane, Bigeye, or a self-hosted variant. The data platform team (not on this ladder) owns it. The tool is deployed, instrumented, and alerts an on-call rotation already.

You must know this landscape for three reasons:

1. **Non-duplication.** When the data platform tool already watches `freshness`, `volume`, and `schema` on a warehouse table backing your pipeline, authoring a parallel `whylogs` metric for the same number is waste.
2. **Where general tools fall short.** The surface you care about — distribution drift at document level, population balance by language, residual-PII rate per source, benchmark contamination hits — is **not** what the warehouse-first tools were built to cover. Chapter 01's distinction between "classical DWH monitoring" and "dataset-side monitoring" is operationalised in this chapter.
3. **Integration shape.** Even when general tools are the wrong *primary* substrate, they are often the right *sink* for your per-slice metrics — the warehouse-first alerting routes, incident timeline, and lineage integration are worth consuming, not reinventing.

This chapter is the field guide. It names the four platforms, maps their coverage, and draws the boundary where your dataset-side contract must do something they do not.

## The four platforms, briefly

### Monte Carlo (<https://www.montecarlodata.com/>)

The platform that coined "data observability" as a product category. SaaS, warehouse-first (Snowflake, BigQuery, Databricks, Redshift, with S3/GCS Lake and lakehouse connectors expanding). Five pillars pitched: *freshness, volume, schema, distribution, lineage*. Distribution monitors are ML-trained anomaly detectors over column statistics, not drift tests between two datasets.

Strong at: autogenerated monitors on every table it discovers; field-level lineage; incident workflows with CRM-shaped resolution; alert routing (PagerDuty, Slack, Microsoft Teams, webhook).

Shape: SaaS tenant, agent or query-based connectors, declarative + auto-generated monitors, incident UI.

### Soda Cloud (<https://www.soda.io/>)

Hybrid between the OSS primitive (Soda Core — covered already in [`mod-104`](../mod-104-data-quality-measurement-and-audit/README.md) Chapter 02) and the hosted platform (Soda Cloud). The OSS side gives you SodaCL (Soda Check Language), a YAML DSL for quality checks; the Cloud side gives you a metric store, dashboard, scheduled runs, and alerting.

Strong at: human-readable check language; first-class `group by` disaggregated-counter block; CI-friendly `soda scan` command; both self-hosted (Soda Core) and SaaS story.

Shape: checks live in your repo as YAML; a Soda Agent or a CI step runs them; Cloud stores results and routes alerts.

### Metaplane (<https://www.metaplane.dev/>)

SaaS data-observability platform, warehouse-first (Snowflake, BigQuery, Redshift, Databricks, Postgres). Pitched as "data observability in minutes" — ML-driven auto-monitoring of row count, null rate, uniqueness, numeric distribution, categorical distribution, freshness, schema. Slack-first alert routing and incident workflows.

Strong at: time-to-value (auto-monitors spin up on discovery); Slack-native incident loop; low-friction setup.

Shape: SaaS tenant, warehouse connection, auto-monitors with a declarative override.

### Bigeye (<https://www.bigeye.com/>)

SaaS data-observability platform, warehouse-first (Snowflake, BigQuery, Databricks, Redshift, SQL Server, Oracle). Pitched around 70+ built-in **"autometrics"** covering freshness, volume, distribution, schema, and value validity. Issues tracker + issue triage workflow.

Strong at: deep SQL coverage; the autometric catalogue; SLA / issue management.

Shape: SaaS, SQL-first, autometric catalogue plus custom-SQL metrics.

### Others you will hear about

- **Acceldata** — unified observability including compute + data; more enterprise-platform-shaped.
- **Lightup** — warehouse-first, SQL-first.
- **Databand (IBM)** — pipeline-run observability (closer to orchestrator telemetry).
- **Anomalo** — ML-first anomaly detection on warehouse tables.
- **Great Expectations Cloud** — hosted side of the OSS GE project; sits closer to Soda Cloud in shape.

## Where the four tools' coverage overlaps and where it diverges

The common ground — what all four tools do well, on tabular warehouse data:

| Capability | Monte Carlo | Soda Cloud | Metaplane | Bigeye | Evidently / whylogs |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Freshness (last-updated-at SLA) | ✅ | ✅ | ✅ | ✅ | not idiomatic |
| Volume (row-count anomaly) | ✅ | ✅ | ✅ | ✅ | ✅ (counts/n) |
| Schema (column set / dtype change) | ✅ | ✅ | ✅ | ✅ | ✅ (via constraints) |
| Null / missing-value rate | ✅ | ✅ | ✅ | ✅ | ✅ |
| Numeric distribution (mean, stddev, quantiles) | ✅ | ✅ | ✅ | ✅ | ✅ |
| Categorical distribution | ✅ | ✅ | ✅ | ✅ | ✅ |
| Uniqueness / duplicate count | ✅ | ✅ | ✅ | ✅ | ✅ |
| Field-level lineage | ✅ | partial | partial | partial | not covered |
| Incident timeline / resolution UI | ✅ | ✅ | ✅ | ✅ | partial (platform-side) |

The general tools cover the above **better** than the ML-aware tools for the use case they were built for: warehouse tables, SQL-first, aggregate statistics, warehouse-native scheduling.

The divergence — what the general tools do *not* cover natively, and what the ML-aware tools (or a hand-rolled layer) do:

| Capability | General DQ Tools | ML-aware Tools |
|---|---|---|
| Reference-vs-current distribution drift (KS, JSD, PSI) on arbitrary slices | partial — most offer "anomaly vs trailing window", not "vs pinned reference" | native (Chapters 02, 03, 05) |
| Text-column drift (length distribution, descriptor drift, embedding drift) | not idiomatic | native in Evidently |
| Per-slice sketched profiles as portable, mergeable artifacts | not available | native in `whylogs` |
| Subpopulation / disaggregated-balance drift (language × TLD cells) | expressible via group-by SQL, rarely built-in | native in Evidently's `ColumnSummaryMetric` by segment |
| Residual-PII rate per source after a redaction stage | custom SQL metric at best | authored directly from [`mod-110`](../mod-110-pii-and-privacy-risk-for-training-data/README.md) outputs |
| Benchmark contamination overlap (n-gram hit, Min-K% Prob) | not supported | custom pipeline step from [`mod-106`](../mod-106-benchmark-contamination-and-decontamination/README.md) |
| Dedup-rate per source family (MinHash-LSH cluster-size shift) | not supported | custom pipeline step from [`mod-105`](../mod-105-deduplication-at-scale/README.md) |
| Lineage integration on Parquet-on-S3 / lakeFS / Pachyderm snapshots | partial via lake connectors; weaker than warehouse | native via OpenLineage ([`mod-109`](../mod-109-dataset-versioning-lineage-reproducibility/README.md) Chapter 06) |
| Dataset version-pin attachment to the alert | not a first-class concept | authored directly in the contract |

The pattern: **general DQ tools cover the aggregate warehouse surface; the AI-pipeline surface carries additional obligations that the general tools do not express**. Chapter 01's distinction is not academic — it is the operational boundary.

## Where general tools fit in your pipeline

Three healthy roles for a Monte Carlo / Soda Cloud / Metaplane / Bigeye instance in a training-data pipeline:

### 1. As the publish-target warehouse monitor

If your published slice is exposed as a Delta / Iceberg table on a warehouse (common pattern when the model team pulls training data from BigQuery or Snowflake), the general tool watches **that table**. Freshness, volume, schema, null-rate, categorical-distribution monitors are auto-generated. You consume those monitors; you do not re-author them in `whylogs`.

In the contract, you cite the general tool's monitor id:

```yaml
- id: m.schema.column_set.v1
  kind: schema_drift
  tool: monte_carlo
  tool_monitor_id: mc.monitor.cc_en_v3.schema
  …
```

The source of truth is the general tool; the contract is the *index* to it.

### 2. As the alerting + incident substrate

Even when the primary computation is `whylogs` or Evidently, the alert routing, incident timeline, acknowledgement, and post-mortem UI are areas the general tools polished for the warehouse use case. Feeding an Evidently or `whylogs` metric into Monte Carlo (via custom monitor) or Soda Cloud (via the metric store API) lets you reuse their routing and incident workflow.

The seam is a "custom metric" API in each tool:

- **Monte Carlo:** `Custom SQL Monitor` and `Custom Metric` definitions.
- **Soda Cloud:** `metric store` + SodaCL `metric_check` referencing a pre-computed metric.
- **Metaplane:** Custom SQL monitor, or metric ingestion via the API.
- **Bigeye:** Custom-SQL autometric or the `custom_metric` endpoint.

The dataset-side metric computes in your pipeline; the general tool stores + alerts. This is the most common integration shape.

### 3. As the field-level lineage consumer

Monte Carlo's field-level lineage and Soda Cloud's lineage integration consume OpenLineage events ([`mod-109`](../mod-109-dataset-versioning-lineage-reproducibility/README.md) Chapter 06). When you emit a lineage event for a slice build, the general tool can display *the slice's upstream sources* alongside its monitors. This is a free win — do not route your lineage events anywhere else before you point them at the warehouse-side consumer.

## When a general tool is *not* the right fit

Three situations where you must author the monitor outside the general tool:

1. **The metric does not fit the warehouse table shape.** Residual-PII rate per source family, benchmark-contamination overlap, dedup-rate per MinHash-LSH cluster — all of these are *per-slice* metrics computed off a Parquet shard, not off a table. The general tool has no idiomatic place for them.
2. **The alert must block the publish, not just notify.** General tools fire alerts; they do not sit in the publish job's exit path. A `publish_blocking` severity (Chapter 01) means your pipeline aborts on breach, which is a scripted step in your own job, not a push notification from Monte Carlo.
3. **The reference is a pinned snapshot, not a trailing window.** General-tool auto-monitors compare to a rolling baseline (last 7 days, last 30 days). A *reproducible* drift test against a pinned reference SHA is not what an auto-monitor is built for. Chapter 05 argues for the pinned-reference case when the pipeline is retraining-coupled.

If any of those three apply, the metric lives in your contract and in the ML-aware tool; the general tool is at most a sink.

## Vendor-coupling and data-residency

All four platforms are SaaS by default. For slices subject to data-residency, export control, or counsel-mandated boundary (e.g. a GDPR-anchored EU-only pipeline), pushing even aggregate metrics to a US-hosted tenant is a DPIA question. Mitigations:

- **Use the self-hosted option where offered.** Soda Core is pure OSS; a self-hosted Soda project server (coming in various enterprise shapes) can keep metrics on-premise.
- **Push aggregate-only, no raw data.** Most general tools support query-result-only ingestion (the agent runs in your environment, only the metric values leave). Confirm per vendor in the current docs.
- **Keep the per-slice profile in your own metric store.** `whylogs` profiles are small; self-hosting a time-series store for them is tractable.
- **Document the choice in the contract.** The contract's `substrate_owner:` field and `data_residency:` note live in the reviewer-reviewed YAML so counsel sees the decision.

## Choosing: a decision matrix for a new pipeline

When starting a fresh pipeline, this is the decision you will walk through. Each row maps to a question for the pipeline lead and the substrate owner.

| Question | If yes → | If no → |
|---|---|---|
| Does the data platform team already run one of the four tools on the published warehouse table? | Consume their monitors for freshness / volume / schema / null-rate. | Author schema + volume monitors yourself in the contract. |
| Is the slice published as a warehouse table or as Parquet-on-object-store? | Warehouse tool coverage is natural. | ML-aware tool (whylogs / Evidently) is natural. |
| Do you need text-descriptor drift or embedding drift? | ML-aware tool, no substitute. | A general tool can cover it. |
| Do you need residual-PII, benchmark-contamination, or dedup-rate monitoring? | Author yourself from [`mod-105`](../mod-105-deduplication-at-scale/README.md) / [`mod-106`](../mod-106-benchmark-contamination-and-decontamination/README.md) / [`mod-110`](../mod-110-pii-and-privacy-risk-for-training-data/README.md) outputs. | Not applicable. |
| Does any alert need to **block** the publish? | Scripted gate in your pipeline job, with the general tool as a notification sink. | Push-only alert via the general tool is fine. |
| Are you subject to a data-residency constraint on metrics? | Self-host (Soda Core, Evidently UI, self-hosted `whylogs` profile store). | SaaS is a reasonable default. |

Walk the matrix once per pipeline; record the answers in the contract's preamble.

## Summary

- Monte Carlo, Soda Cloud, Metaplane, and Bigeye are the four general data-observability platforms you will meet. Each is warehouse-first.
- Their strong surface — freshness, volume, schema, nulls, numeric and categorical distribution, lineage, incident UI — overlaps with what you need, but it is *the warehouse-table surface*, not the training-data-pipeline surface.
- The AI-pipeline surface (distribution drift vs pinned reference, text descriptors / embedding drift, residual-PII rate, benchmark-contamination overlap, dedup-rate per family, population balance per subpopulation) is not natively covered.
- Three healthy integration shapes: the general tool as warehouse-side watcher (you consume), as the alerting substrate (you compute, it routes), and as the lineage consumer (you emit, it displays).
- Data-residency and vendor-coupling considerations may push you to self-hosted / OSS variants. Document the decision in the contract.
- Write a decision matrix once per pipeline. Do not re-author monitors the platform team already runs.
